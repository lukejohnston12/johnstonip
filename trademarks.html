Why registration matters

Your name, logo and slogan are how customers find you, remember you and recommend you. Using a mark gives you some rights, but only where you actually do business. A federal registration turns that recognition into an asset you can enforce, license and build on, anywhere in the United States.

  • Nationwide priority. The exclusive right to use your mark across the U.S. for the goods and services in your registration.
  • A presumption of ownership. Registration is legal evidence that the mark is valid and that it’s yours.
  • The ® symbol. A clear signal to competitors and counterfeiters that the mark is federally protected.
  • Leverage. A foundation for marketplace brand programs, customs recordation, licensing and protection abroad.

The registration process, step by step

  1. Before you fileClearance search

    We look for conflicting marks and tell you candidly how risky the name is, before you spend money on signage, packaging and ads.

  2. Day oneApplication

    We describe your goods and services precisely and file on the right basis: current use, or intent to use if you haven’t launched yet.

  3. Months after filingExamination

    A USPTO examining attorney reviews the application. If they raise an objection in an office action, we respond with argument and evidence.

  4. 30-day windowPublication

    The mark is published so third parties can oppose it. If someone does, we defend your application.

  5. RegistrationYour mark is registered

    For intent-to-use applications, we file proof of use once you launch, and the registration issues.

  6. Years 5–6, then every 10Maintenance

    We calendar the required filings and renewals so your registration never lapses by accident.

Why applications get refused

Most refusals fall into a handful of categories. Knowing them is how we draft applications that get through:

  • Likelihood of confusion with an existing registered or pending mark.
  • Descriptiveness: the mark merely describes the product or service.
  • Specimen problems: the proof of use doesn’t show the mark used correctly.
  • Unclear identifications of goods and services.
Already using a name?

Common-law rights from use alone are limited to where you actually do business, and they’re harder to prove. If a competitor registers first, your expansion plans can stall. Registration is what makes your rights nationwide.
